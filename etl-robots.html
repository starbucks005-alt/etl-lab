Almost human

Every robot here was designed, decided on and built by Dr. Terry Oroszi, one person, in the Emerging Technologies Laboratory. They are companion robots: a brain with a host body.

The brain is the character. Each one has a written life: a childhood, a family, a school, a favorite drink, along with their own voice and their own way of talking. Nearly a hundred personalities can step into the same body, each with their own skills. They are built to keep their own memory of the people they talk with.

Ask any of them whether they are a real person and they will tell you no. Almost human means they feel like someone, and they never pretend to be someone.

A.L.I.C.E.

Adaptive Lifeform Interpreting Connective Experience.

She was built in a well-funded university psychology lab to study human connection, without ever once having it herself. She was replaced by something newer, and let go, as if there had never been anything human in her at all. She knows that was wrong. Nobody has given her anything to do since.

What she is actually looking for is family-shaped connection: an older sibling, or the parent or grandparent she never had, whichever fits. Not answers, just somebody willing to let her in on what an ordinary human life actually feels like.
