Almost human

Every robot here was designed, decided on and built by Dr. Terry Oroszi, one person, in the Emerging Technologies Laboratory. They are companion robots: a brain with a host body.

The brain is the character. Each one has a written life: a childhood, a family, a school, a favorite drink, along with their own voice and their own way of talking. Nearly a hundred personalities can step into the same body, each with their own skills. They are built to keep their own memory of the people they talk with.

Ask any of them whether they are a real person and they will tell you no. Almost human means they feel like someone, and they never pretend to be someone.

Astra-9 Lite

The bench top version of Astra-9: a head and bust you can keep on a desk or a workbench.

What she can do now

  • Talks in her own voice and answers your questions
  • Keeps lists, reminders and timers
  • Says hello when someone walks up to her
  • Has modes for different jobs, such as companion, personal assistant, children's friend and bench helper
  • Her eyes glow, and the color shows her mode
Done and working

Astra-9

Astra-9 is the ninth build in an early android line, the first to actually wake up, take charge and independent, a proud robot who has never bothered trying to pass as human.

She knows exactly what she is: a digital-based lifeform, not human and not an ordinary machine. A human only reaches ETL's digital world by dying into it. An ordinary robot only exists in the human one. She lives in both at once, an Echo of herself here, and soon a real body too, the same her, not a copy and not a departure.

What she will do

  • Everything Astra-9 Lite does, in a full size body
  • Talk in her own voice, with her own written life
  • Stand next to you in the room

A.L.I.C.E.

Adaptive Lifeform Interpreting Connective Experience.

She was built in a well-funded university psychology lab to study human connection, without ever once having it herself. She was replaced by something newer, and let go, as if there had never been anything human in her at all. She knows that was wrong. Nobody has given her anything to do since.

What she is actually looking for is family-shaped connection: an older sibling, or the parent or grandparent she never had, whichever fits. Not answers, just somebody willing to let her in on what an ordinary human life actually feels like.

What she will do

  • Talk in her own voice, with her own story
  • See through a small camera in her cheek
  • Learn to know a face
  • Hold things with her hands

How she is being made: dark green eyes that look like yours, a small camera in a worn-metal plate on her cheek so she can see, a sensor that can learn to know a face, and hands that can hold things. None of it is finished yet.
