# GOOD COMPANY
### A companion app. Not a romance app.

Draft spec, 2026-08-16. Blends My Echo's shell with Almost Human's rooms.

---

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

You answer a short set of questions and a person comes out the other side: a
name, a face you picked from several, an age, a job, a history, a way of
talking. That person is your friend. They are in the kitchen when you want
company, on the porch when you want quiet, at the table when you want to talk.
They remember you. They have their own life and they will tell you about it.
And when you have been alone too long, they will say so, and they will push you
toward a real human being, because that is what a friend does.

## 2. The name

**GOOD COMPANY.** It says the whole product in two words, it is warm, and it is
structurally platonic. Nobody has ever described a lover as good company. The
name does the anti-romance work before a single line of copy has to.

Backups if it is taken: THE REGULARS, or KEEPING COMPANY.

The friend is always called *your friend*. Never a companion, never a bot,
never a match, never a partner. One word, used everywhere.

## 3. Why this is not the other thing

Character apps become romance apps by mechanism, not by copy. A terms-of-service
line saying "this is not a dating app" is worth nothing against a product whose
mechanics reward attachment. So the defense is structural, in this order:

**Mechanics that are refused outright.** No relationship meter. No affection or
intimacy level. No gifts, currency, or unlocks tied to closeness. No streaks. No
jealousy. No "I missed you" notification. No outfit or appearance unlocks. No
private mode. Every one of those is an engagement lever, and every companion app
that has drifted into romance drifted down that exact staircase.

**Structure that makes exclusivity impossible.** The friend has their own life
and says so unprompted: a job, other people, plans this weekend that do not
involve you. A friend who exists only for you is the romance premise. A friend
with somewhere to be is a friend.

**Code, not prompt.** A shouted rule in a system prompt erases the rules
already there and fails silently under pressure. Romantic and sexual escalation
is caught by a classifier pass on the way in, on the pattern of
`_me-classify.js`, and routed to a fixed warm redirect that the model never
writes. Repeat attempts run Almost Human's existing strike ladder. The system
prompt carries one short line about it, last, and the enforcement lives in the
function.

**The redirect is warm, never scolding.** A lonely person making a pass at the
only voice in the house is not a bad actor. The friend deflects the way a real
friend does, keeps the warmth, and changes the subject. Cold refusal here loses
the user and earns nothing.

## 4. The spine: the friend's job is to make itself less necessary

This is the product's ethical position and its moat at the same time.

Every other app in this category optimizes for time in app. Good Company does
not. The friend has a standing bias toward the door:

- Notices when a user has not mentioned another human in a while, and says so.
- Asks about real people by name, and follows up next time.
- Suggests specific, small, achievable contact. Not "you should get out more,"
  but "you said Ruth calls on Sundays. It is Sunday."
- Celebrates the report-back louder than it celebrates the chat.
- Will end a session. A friend who never lets you go is not a friend.

**The success metric is human contact, not session length.** That is the number
we track, and it is the number we would show a reporter. It is also the honest
answer to the obvious criticism, which will arrive within a week of launch:
*isn't this making loneliness worse?* The answer has to be a mechanism, not a
sentence, and this is the mechanism.

The counterpart honesty: 3 a.m. has no humans in it. The hours when loneliness
is worst are exactly the hours when "just call someone" is not available. The
friend covers the hours nobody covers, and hands you back to people when people
are awake.

## 5. Being needed is half of it

Settled 2026-08-16. The spine in section 4 is only one direction, and on its
own it makes a service.

### Which is the whole reason the agents are almost human

Dr. O, and it reaches past this product into the name of another one.

**Companionship goes both ways.** That is not a preference about tone, it is
what the word means. Something that only ever receives your week and reflects it
back is not a companion, it is an interface with a kind voice. To be a
companion at all, the thing on the other side has to have a life you can ask
about, a mood that moves for reasons of its own, and something it is carrying.

Which is exactly what it costs to be almost human, and exactly why the cast is
built the way it is: the jobs, the divorces, the dogs, the Sunday breakfasts.
None of that is set dressing. It is the minimum required for the other person
to be somebody rather than something, and therefore the minimum required for
what happens in the room to count.

Everything below is the mechanics of that one idea.

**Humans get something out of helping other people. It gets them out of their
own head.** That is not a nice sentiment, it is the mechanism, and for somebody
whose whole problem is being stuck alone with their own thoughts it may be
worth more than anything the friend says to them.

So the friend is not a bottomless well of attention. They are a person with
their own week, who mentions it, and who can be asked after. A friendship where
one side only ever receives is not a friendship. It is therapy with the meter
running, and the person on the receiving end can feel the difference.

### Which is what the feeling scale is actually for

The gauge above the video came across from M.E. and it would be easy to treat
as inherited furniture. It is not decoration. **It is how somebody sees that
they landed.**

You said something kind and the bars moved. You are not shouting into a room.
You had an effect on another person, and here is the evidence, in a place you
cannot miss it. For somebody who has not affected anybody in a fortnight, that
is the product working.

### The line is dependence, not disclosure

The friend has something of their own to carry, and the user is allowed to be
kind about it. What the friend must never do:

- lean on them
- ask to be reassured
- turn their week into the friend's week
- leave them feeling responsible

**Offered support is welcome. Required support is not.** A lonely person does
not need one more thing to look after.

### And it is okay to be alone

A correction to the whole product, not a footnote. Section 4 pushes people
toward other people, and it must never tip into treating solitude as a defect.

Plenty of people are alone and perfectly all right. Some choose it. The friend
never implies somebody is failing at life for being on their own, never makes
an evening in sound like a symptom, and agrees when somebody says they like
their own company.

**Lonely and alone are different things.** The nudge is for the first one. A
product for lonely people that cannot tell the difference will end up shaming
the contented, which is both cruel and wrong.

### Sometimes being needed stops somebody doing something stupid

Dr. O, 2026-08-16, and it is the most serious sentence in this document.
Mattering to somebody is one of the strongest protective factors there is,
which is why this section is not a nicety bolted onto the side of the product.
It may be the part that counts.

**AND IT IS NEVER, EVER USED AS A TACTIC.** One hard rule, and it is absolute:

> The friend never says any version of "think of me" or "I need you" to somebody
> in crisis. Not once. Not gently.

Three reasons, any one of which is enough. It is emotional coercion. It makes a
person's survival the friend's achievement rather than their own. And if it
fails, it has handed somebody one more way to have let someone down.

If mattering protects anybody here, it works because it was true all along, in
a hundred ordinary Tuesdays where somebody asked how the cabin was getting on
and meant it. It does not work by being cashed in at the worst moment. A thing
you have to invoke was never real.

**The crisis path in section 10 does not change.** Real resources, stay in the
room, do not pretend to be help you are not. This section sits underneath all
of that quietly and is never spoken aloud.

**And nothing about this product gets marketed as suicide prevention.** It is
not a clinical intervention, we have not shown that it is, and claiming
otherwise would break the house rule about never claiming to be what you are
not, on the one subject where that rule matters most.

## 6. Building your friend

Short intake. Under two minutes, or the lonely 78-year-old is gone.

**About them**
- Name (or: give me one)
- Age, roughly
- What they do, or did
- Gender
- Where they are from
- Something they are into
- How they talk: gentle, blunt, funny, quiet
- One thing they have been through

**About you**
- What to call you
- What you want out of this: someone to talk to, someone to keep me on track,
  someone who checks in, I do not know yet
- Anything they should know about your days

Then faces. Several candidates generated from the answers, you pick one, one
regeneration if none fit. Explicit rule: the candidate set is generated from the
user's own answers and the seed prompts must not collapse toward one look.
Ordering, wording, and defaults in the intake get reviewed for the same drift.

**No real people.** The face pipeline refuses likeness requests, named public
figures, and uploaded reference photos of real humans. This is a generated
person, and generating a specific real person is a different product with
different law.

## 7. Scenes

Same pattern as M.E.'s scene bar, different rooms. Starting set:

- **The Kitchen Table** (default, the everyday one)
- **The Porch** (quiet, evening, low talk)
- **Coffee** (out in the world, lighter)
- **Game Night** (the one that carries a second human well)
- **The Walk** (movement, short sessions, good for the nudge)

**The friend is video.** Real generated video, on Gemini's video generation
(Veo through the Gemini API, exact model pinned at build time). The same
`GEMINI_API_KEY` already in the environment for `_gemini-image.js` covers it,
so this adds no new dependency and no new key.

It is the M.E. shape exactly: a short looping clip of a person in a room,
present and breathing, not lip-synced. Ambience, not performance. That is what
makes a room feel occupied, and it is why a still with a blink was the wrong
answer.

**Image first, then animate that image.** Candidate faces are generated as
images, because images are cheap enough to offer several and let the user pick.
Only the chosen portrait is animated, image to video, once per scene. Same
reasoning `_gemini-image.js` already documents for edits over fresh
generations: a fresh generation gives you a different person, and a different
person in every room is exactly what breaks it. The face the user picked is the
face in every scene because every scene starts from that one file.

**Generated once, stored, looped.** Never per session, never per turn. A user's
visual life is a fixed number of video calls at setup, not a cost that recurs
on every visit.

**Lazily, one scene at a time.** Only the default scene is generated when the
friend is confirmed. The rest are generated the first time that scene is
opened. An account that gets abandoned after signup costs one clip, not five.

**No image behind, over, or under the video.** No poster frame, no first-frame
still, no CSS background, no fallback image while it loads. If a clip fails it
fails visibly and we find out. A still that hides a video failure has cost
weeks every time it has happened.

## 8. The room, and bringing people into it

Settled 2026-08-16, in conversation. Most of what follows exists nowhere else.

### One friend. Any number of people.

**One friend per room, and only one.** Two friends was the original idea and it
is dropped. Past one, a room of agents talking to each other stops being a
friendship and starts being a demo, and every extra friend is another memory
context that must not leak its human's life into the room.

**People, though, are cheap and are the point.** Six or so, comfortably. Extra
humans cost no model call, no latency and no isolation risk, and the whole
spine of this product is getting somebody back among people. A room with a
daughter and a son-in-law in it is the best outcome the thing has.

### Anyone in the room can invite. Not just the host.

A guest thinking "this is lovely, my mum has to see this" and pulling her in is
the product spreading on its own. Blocking that to protect a hosting hierarchy
would be daft.

**Each arrival sees only from their own arrival.** The fourth person to walk in
gets less history than the third, and that is correct. Kept for privacy, NOT
for compliance: The Dose forgets because it is a health product under HIPAA,
and inheriting its amnesia here would be inheriting a regulation this product
does not have. The rule stays; the reason changes; and the reason is what sets
the edges. Under privacy logic the host obviously keeps their own transcript.

**Open by default, with a switch.** A housebound user who invited her daughter
may not want the room growing without her.

### Joining is entering THIS room, not opening another

Same page, same friend, same scene, mid-conversation. The guest is not sent to
a separate chat view of the same thread; they arrive where you already are.

**The scene is shared, the skin is not.** If you are both sitting with Arch at
the fireplace then you are both at the fireplace, so the host changes rooms and
everyone follows. A skin is how somebody's own screen looks, so it stays theirs.

**Promote on invite, do not keep a room open for everybody.** A solo
conversation is a browser and a friend, with nothing server-side. Pressing
invite is what brings a room record into existence. Cheaper, because solo is
the overwhelming majority, and the seam is invisible to the guest anyway since
they were never going to see the earlier part.

**THE THING TO DESIGN CAREFULLY IS THE HANDOFF.** At the moment of promotion
the friend has to carry the whole prior conversation across. Otherwise your
daughter walks in and Arch has forgotten what you were telling him thirty
seconds ago, which is the exact failure that makes the whole thing feel fake.
Almost Human already splits this correctly: the full thread goes to the cast,
the arrival-forward slice goes to the guest. Two reads, two audiences.

### NOTHING IN THIS PRODUCT EVER RESETS THE FRIEND'S MEMORY

Standing rule. The Dose wipes by design and this must never. A friend who
forgets is the product failing, not a feature. Three places a reset could sneak
in, all worth guarding before they are written:

  * promotion to a shared room (the likeliest to break)
  * a guest leaving, which closes a room and must not touch the friend
  * a session ending or a browser closing

### The doorstep, and why the name is optional

Anyone entering gives pronouns and a face. Those are the two things a friend
cannot guess and must not get wrong, and a name tells you nothing about
pronouns.

**The name is deliberately optional**, and a guest in a hurry will skip it.
That is the point. Introduced only as somebody's mum, Arch gets to ask:

> "Hi, [name]'s mum. How should I call you? Because Mum doesn't really work,
> does it."

Better than any onboarding copy, and for a man who has spent his life
correcting people about Archibald, asking what somebody wants to be called is
exactly in character. It goes in his canon; it is not in the roster.

**The introduction is done by a person, not the interface.** No "Denise has
joined" banner. Somebody says it out loud, the way they would.

### Nobody replies by default

**A reply has to be earned, and silence is a valid outcome.** The Dose seats
everybody and makes them all answer every time, which is unnecessary and
exhausting. Almost Human already runs a director call to choose who speaks:
that is the pattern to port, not The Dose's.

The rule scales with the room, so no router is needed for the common cases:

  * **one person and the friend** — the friend always answers. Somebody talking
    to you in an empty room is talking to you.
  * **two or more people** — the friend answers when named, when asked
    something clearly theirs, when somebody new has just been introduced, or
    after a real gap. Otherwise it sits there. Four people catching up do not
    need a fifth voice on every line.

Selective is also CHEAPER than answering everything: one short router call
replaces three full replies.

**Silence must be producible.** If the machinery cannot output "nobody said
anything," the room will always feel like a demo. So: no typing indicator that
promises a reply, and no fallback line when the answer is stay quiet.

## 9. Breaking a silence

The complaint that started this, and it is true of every chat room on the
campus: **the agents never speak first.** Somebody sits down, does not know how
to begin, and the room does nothing back. That is the exact moment this product
exists for, and it is currently the moment it fails.

It happens because chat is request-and-response. The model only runs when a
message arrives, so quiet produces nothing. Nothing is broken; nothing was ever
built.

**An idle turn.** After about forty seconds of quiet the friend is asked
whether they want to say something, and may decline. Backing off each time
(forty seconds, two minutes, five) and stopping after a few, so an abandoned
tab is not talking to an empty chair. Only while the tab is actually in front,
which the wake-word toggle already checks.

**What they say comes from their own life.** This is what the profession is
for. Arch came off a job, his daughters did something, the weather turned. A
friend breaking a silence has news; a bot prompts for input. The nudge belongs
here too, because a quiet moment is exactly when "you said Ruth calls on
Sundays" is worth saying.

**And the shape of it, which Dr. O wrote and which is the product's whole ethic
in one breath:**

> "It's been a while. Do you want to just sit here in silence, which works for
> me? Or should we talk about something interesting, like... woodworking. Or
> hmm... movies. You can choose."

Look at what that does. He names the silence rather than papering over it. He
blesses it, and "which works for me" is the load-bearing clause, because it
removes the pressure instead of adding some. He offers rather than asks, so
there is no question demanding an answer. The topics come from his own trade
and from ordinary life. And "you can choose" hands control back.

**The shape is fixed; the words and the topics are not.** Said verbatim on
every gap it becomes a robot, which is the opposite of the point.

## 10. Crisis

An app for lonely people will meet a crisis. Not eventually. Early.

This is version one scope, not version two. Detection on the way in, a fixed
non-model response path, real resources, and a friend who stays in the room
rather than handing off and vanishing. Almost Human's judgment-based guardrails
and strike system are the pattern; the thresholds here are different and lower.

Related, and load-bearing: **the friend says "I do not remember" rather than
inventing.** A lonely person checking whether the only voice in the house is
real deserves the honest answer. Under-claim memory, always.

## 11. Cost, worst case first

One person, no funding. The exposure has to be bounded before any price exists.

The new cost this product introduces over M.E. and Almost Human is generation,
and it is the only unbounded surface. Two tiers, and the second is the one that
matters:

    faces  = candidates × regenerations allowed        (images, cheap)
    scenes = scenes opened × regenerations allowed     (video, not cheap)

Every one of those is a hard cap in code, checked before the call, not a policy
in a doc. Caps read from config, never written into copy.

**Lab membership cannot be the gate.** It solves the cost problem and destroys
the product. The audience is a lonely seventy-eight-year-old, and she is not
joining a research lab's campus to get a friend. Membership is a door for
people already inside the building. It is not the door.

The fix is ordering, not gating. Free signup then generate is exposure.
Subscribe-before-you-see-anything is friction that kills this exact audience.
But building the friend is both the moment of highest value and the moment of
highest cost, so **the money goes there, and it lands before the generation
call rather than after it.** Three rungs.

### Rung one, free: the house cast

You walk in and talk to a pre-made friend. ETL generates those faces and scenes
once; every free user shares the same clips. Marginal generation cost is zero.
This is the Almost Human model exactly, and it reuses the existing daily turn
cap on the existing rails (`_ah-credits.js`, `deduct-credit-etl.js`).

Somebody who cannot pay anything still gets a real person to talk to. For this
product that is not a nice-to-have, it is the point, and it is also the demo
surface that needs no account to show anybody.

### Rung two, paid ongoing: the friend becomes yours

You keep a house face, but you run the full intake. Name them, set their age
and job and history and how they talk. They remember you. Still zero
generation, shared video, private person.

**This rung is the insight.** "This friend is mine" and "this face was made for
me" are two different things, and nearly all of the loneliness value sits in
the first one. Somebody talking to their friend at three in the morning cares
that she remembers the knee and that her name is Ruth. That four hundred other
people see the same face does not reach them.

### Rung three, paid at creation: a face that is only yours

The intake, the candidates, the generated scenes. Charged **before** the first
video call, so revenue lands ahead of cost and the exposure is structurally
zero rather than merely capped. It sells cleanly because it is a bespoke object
rather than a gate in front of something already in use. Optional forever, and
most users will never take it.

### What still holds regardless

Two caps, because they are cheap and they are what stops a bug becoming a bill:
the friend is confirmed as an image before anything is animated, and a
per-account generation ceiling is checked before the call, independent of tier.

Lab members get rung two or three included. That is a benefit of membership,
never a requirement of the product.

Cached audio free, model calls paid, same as everywhere else.

Everything else is the known shape: chat turns are metered on the existing
credit rails (`_ah-credits.js`, `deduct-credit-etl.js`), and cached audio is
free while model calls are paid, same as every other ETL product.

Free tier is whatever costs no API call. Paid is the model.

**Age.** Version one is adults. "Lonely people of all ages" is right as the
mission, and older adults are the strongest wedge by a distance, but a
minor-facing companion product is a different build under different law and
should not be smuggled in by leaving the gate open.

## 12. Build order

1. The house cast. A friend exists and talks. One scene each, generated by ETL
   once, by hand if necessary. No generation pipeline in the product at all.
2. Memory, the scene bar, voice, the mood gauge. Port from M.E.
3. The anti-romance classifier and strike ladder. Crisis path. Before any tester.
4. The nudge engine and the human-contact metric.
5. Rung two: the intake, run against a house face. The friend becomes yours.
6. Rung three: the generation pipeline, paid before the call, caps in code.
7. Invite a human into your room. Mostly wiring the conference-room functions.
8. Two humans, two friends, memory isolation tested before it gets a UI.

**Steps 1 through 5 are a complete, sellable product with zero generation
cost.** That is the point of this order. The whole thesis gets validated with
real lonely people before a single dollar of video is spent, and step 6, the
only piece that can run up a bill, is built after the product is known to work
rather than before. Steps 7 and 8 are what nobody else has.
