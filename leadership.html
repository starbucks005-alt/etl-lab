The Ten Leaders

Visionary, pacesetting, coaching, coalition, nonviolent, crisis, persuasive: real leadership theory taught by the people who actually lived it. Each portrait below is tagged with the style that leader teaches.

Eleanor Roosevelt

Visionary Leadership

Refused to trust a single source, and helped the world agree on its first shared list of human rights.

Marie Curie

Pacesetting Leadership

A personal standard so relentless it won two Nobel Prizes, and cost her, and her daughter, their health.

John Wooden

Coaching Leadership

Won ten national championships by refusing to mention winning at all.

Frances Perkins

Democratic & Coalition Leadership

The first woman in a U.S. Cabinet, who spent twelve years building the coalition that created Social Security.

Mohandas Gandhi

Affiliative & Nonviolent Leadership

Turned truth and self-suffering into a weapon strong enough to move an empire.

Coretta Scott King

Affiliative & Nonviolent Leadership

A movement leader and institution builder in her own right, not only Martin's wife.

Martin Luther King Jr.

Nonviolent & Persuasive Leadership

Turned the discipline of nonviolence into a strategy that could win, and stayed in dialogue with people who wanted him silenced.

Harriet Tubman

Crisis & Commanding Leadership

Never lost a passenger, and never let one turn back.

Ernest Shackleton

Crisis & Commanding Leadership

The goal changed the moment the ice took the ship. Getting every man home became the only mission that mattered.

Sojourner Truth

Persuasive Rhetoric

Turned her own body and story into unanswerable argument.

Course Schedule

PTX 7006, Leadership Theory and Application. Fifteen weeks, built around the ten leaders above plus assigned readings from Dr. Oroszi's Forbes Technology Council series on AI and human judgment.

Learning Objectives

  • Gain insight on how to lead others, particularly individuals who function differently than themselves.
  • Learn how to give bad news with kindness.
  • Pay equal attention to verbal and nonverbal cues to lead more effectively.
  • Develop public speaking skills that leave students always prepared.

Learning Outcomes

  • Learn how to self-evaluate, the first step to becoming a successful leader.
  • Understand how AI agents and decision-support tools are entering leadership practice, and build sound judgment about when to rely on them and when not to.
  • Prepare for long-term professional success.

About this classroom. Ten real historical leaders, each teaching a distinct, real leadership style, and a professionally-scoped faculty twin for course-material questions. Every leader agent has genuine, real-time access to sources and never fabricates a fact or a quote; if a lookup fails, they say so plainly.

Some of these leaders knew each other in real life, Eleanor Roosevelt and Frances Perkins were real, longtime friends and allies; Coretta Scott King and Martin Luther King Jr. were married; Martin Luther King Jr. studied Gandhi's writings closely and traveled to India to learn from his followers. Others, like Harriet Tubman and Ernest Shackleton, never met and are paired here purely by theme, for comparing leadership method under extreme stakes. Every agent is built to be honest about exactly which connections are real.
