# ETL YOU brand direction

Yuki Mendel, for relay to Dr. O.

---

BRAND SNAPSHOT:

I looked at the live castle and at the sheet Gemini produced. The castle is in
better shape than the sheet is. The lockup construction is right, ETL's three
bars carried through unchanged with YOU set small against the wordmark, and the
decision to run the child's colour themes on ETL's own palette rather than a
new one is the single best call in the whole build. The sheet is a different
matter. Its specimen text is generated nonsense, the URLs on it are not
registered, and it proposes an activity-card product line that does not exist.
Treat it as a sketch of an idea, not a system.

The real problem is not visual. It is that this brand has two audiences with
opposite requirements and only one surface. A four-year-old navigates by
picture at 34 pixels. Her parent needs to believe this teaches geology. Most
children's brands solve that by being loud for the child and putting the
credibility in the app-store copy. You cannot, because the parent lands on the
same page she does.

TYPOGRAPHY DIRECTION:

Keep Public Sans. It is doing its job, it is ETL, and there is no argument for
changing it.

Spectral is the question. At display sizes it is correct and I would not touch
it: it is what makes the parent take the page seriously, and it is the visible
thread back to ETL. Below about 15 pixels it is the wrong tool. Spectral has a
small x-height and fine hairlines, and you are currently setting wing names in
italic Spectral at 12 pixels under each door. On a phone at arm's length that
is mush, and it is the one line a parent reads to learn that Nerida is marine
science.

So: Spectral for display only, hard floor at 20 pixels. Everything under that
goes to Public Sans, including the wing names, the window captions and the
curriculum table. Two families, four voices, which is the ETL rule already.

If you ever want a third register for the child layer specifically, the honest
answer is that you do not need one. The artwork is carrying that load.

COLOR DIRECTION:

The ETL-palette-with-child-names decision is right and I would build on it, not
around it.

Stamp blue #28527a: primary. Anchors the whole thing and is the reason this
reads as ETL and not as a toy.

Paper #f4f3ee and the sky tints above it: background. Keep the castle light.
The portraits are saturated and painterly and they need somewhere quiet to sit.

Greylander gold #c9a23f: accent, selection, the earned crown. It is already
doing this and it is the correct amount of gold.

Evergreen #35806f: the second theme. It holds up against the artwork better
than any of the tints do.

Ink #16202b: text. Not black.

One caution. ETL's palette is deliberately desaturated, and that is right for
the parent-facing side and slightly thin for a child choosing a favourite
colour. Coral Reef standing in as Petal Pink is the seam showing: it is a
coral, and a four-year-old asking for pink will notice. I would either commit
and add one properly saturated rose to the ETL YOU palette as a sanctioned
extension, or rename it Coral and stop pretending. Do not leave it halfway.

LAYOUT AND HIERARCHY:

The log-in row is the strongest screen in the product. Title, badge, colour,
in one place, all three chosen by the child. Do not let anything else move into
it.

The wing's four-panel grid has the wrong emphasis. The transcript panel is the
largest thing on screen and it is the only panel the child cannot use. The
princess and the game should own the space; the conversation is for the adult
sitting alongside and should read as a margin note, not as the main column.

Ten doors at five across on desktop is two clean rows and correct. On a phone
the pairs are right. Leave it.

The badges are the weak drawing. Star, moon, heart, crown and sun are clean
geometry. The butterfly, the bee and the ladybug are detailed silhouettes
trying to survive at 28 pixels, and detail is exactly what does not survive at
28 pixels. They should be redrawn to the same weight and complexity as the
star, or they will always look like a different set.

RECOMMENDED DELIVERABLES:

1. ETL YOU lockup, light and dark, in SVG, with a minimum size and clear-space
   rule. The construction exists; this makes it a specification.
2. A type specimen sheet showing the Spectral floor at 20 pixels, with the
   substitution rules written down so nobody has to guess.
3. The ten badges redrawn as one family at a single weight, tested at 28
   pixels before anything else.
4. A one-page ETL YOU palette card: the six themes, their ETL sources, and the
   contrast pairs that are safe for small text on each.
5. A sub-brand rule, so the second ETL YOU product does not need this
   conversation again.

One question, and it changes item five. Is Everly Castle the whole of ETL YOU
for the next year, or the first of several? If it is the first of several, I
build a system where each product gets a sub-mark and the parent mark stays
constant. If it is the whole thing, ETL YOU and Everly Castle should collapse
into one identity and you carry half as much.
