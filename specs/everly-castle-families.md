# Everly Castle — family image briefs

For the **My family** button in each wing. The picture appears while the
princess speaks, so what is in the frame has to match what the child is
hearing, word for word. Each brief below quotes that line first: **if the
audio names three people, the picture shows three people.** A child who
hears about a little brother and sees four strangers has been told the
screen does not mean anything, which is the fault this whole build has spent
two days closing.

**House rules for all ten**

- **They are not royalty, and that is the point.** Every girl is a princess:
  it is what she IS, not a rank she was born into. So these are ordinary
  families in real countries, with no crowns, no thrones and no palaces. The
  castle calls every child who arrives Princess before it knows her name,
  and that only means something if princess is a thing you can already be.
- **Ordinary is not the same as plain.** Warm, specific, real households with
  good things in them: a garden somebody actually tends, a kitchen in use, a
  music room full of instruments, a workshop full of half-mended machines.
  Not grand, not poor. Somebody's family.
- **Real in the manner of that country**, not one look ten times. A house in
  Mongolia is not a house in Norway, and the room is half the geography.
- **These are families living NOW, not in history.** Nerida talks about
  dropped ice creams and lemonade bottle tops; her family cannot be in
  chitons and laurel. Modern clothes, modern rooms. The countries in this
  castle are real and current, which is the whole difference between this and
  every other princess app.
- **They must look like they belong in her portrait.** Each princess already
  has one, and it is storybook, colourful and warm. A family rendered as a
  museum diorama beside her reads as a different product.
- **No writing anywhere in the picture.** No names on clothes, no signs, no
  labels. She cannot read, so text is meaningless marks to her.

- **A country is not one face, and this is deliberate.** Posy is Asian and
  French. That is not an error to be corrected in later images, it is the
  point: children who live in France look like all sorts of people, and a
  four-year-old learning what a country is should learn that first, before
  anybody teaches her otherwise.

  And a family does not have to look alike. Adopted, mixed race, second
  generation: all of it is ordinary and all of it belongs here. It is a
  running theme across ETL's whole cast, not a one-off for this product. Bex already
  says it out loud, that her aunt brought her up, and it is the most ordinary
  sentence in her recording.

  **Two rules make this work, and the second one matters more.**

  Be consistent. The same person looks the same in every image of that family,
  so it reads as a decision rather than as a slip.

  **Never remark on it.** No princess explains why her family looks the way it
  does, nobody says "even though we do not look alike", and no picture makes a
  point of it. A child who is adopted, or whose family does not match, should
  meet ten families where this is simply true and nobody mentions it. The
  moment it is explained it becomes a topic, and she is the reason it came up.

- The princess is **in** the picture, matching her portrait, so a
  four-year-old knows whose family this is.
- Set it where they actually live, in their own palace or grounds. The room
  is half the geography lesson.
- Everyone is doing something. No lined-up posed portraits: a five-year-old
  reads a posed row as strangers, however fine their clothes.
- Landscape, and keep faces away from the very bottom edge.
- Names in brackets are not spoken to the child; they are only here so the
  same person looks the same in every image you make later.

**One exception, and it matters.** Elowyn is Maori, and this artwork has
already been revised three times to take out sacred items: korowai, pou, hei
tiki. Do not dress a fantasy monarchy in taonga. Her family is plainly the
most important family in the place, and that should read through bearing,
setting and how they are listened to, not through borrowed regalia.

## Posy — France

> "There are four of us. My brother Luc is big, nineteen, and he does not grow
> anything at all but he tells me how to do my garden anyway. My cousin Margot
> is six and she follows me everywhere and she has her own little trowel and
> she will not let me borrow it, not even once. And my grandmother, who taught
> me all of it."

**Four people.** Posy; **Luc**, 19, tall, clean hands, clearly giving advice;
**Margot**, 6, gripping her own small trowel, right at Posy's elbow; and
**Grandmother**, older, the one who actually knows.
**Setting:** the family garden behind their house in France, rows and a wall and a gravel path. Everybody in ordinary summer clothes and Posy filthy anyway, which is the joke.

## Nerida — Greece

> "There are five of us children and I am in the middle… My big sister Thea has
> gone to live in Athens and I miss her. My little brother Kosta is four and he
> thinks the whole rock pool belongs to him…"

**Five children, no adults.** Nerida in the middle, mid-sentence. **Thea**,
oldest, slightly apart — she is the one who has left. **Kosta**, 4, holding a
bucket he will not surrender. Two more siblings [**Stavros**, 12, and
**Ioanna**, 9] anywhere in frame.
**Setting:** a stone terrace above the rocks with worn steps down to the water. Ordinary Greek summer clothes, five children barefoot on wet stone. The terrace, the light and the bare feet on wet stone are right and worth keeping; the clothes are modern Greek summer, not antiquity. Thea should look like a girl who has moved to Athens, because that is who she is.

## Zephyra — Nepal

> "My brother Dawa is fifteen and he is better at kites than me… My grandmother
> lives downstairs and she shouts up the stairs when I have been on the tower
> too long."

**Three people.** Zephyra; **Dawa**, 15, holding the better kite and knowing
it; **Grandmother**, at the bottom of the stairs, mid-shout, entirely
affectionate.
**Setting:** the roof and courtyard of their house in Nepal, carved wood and brickwork, mountains beyond, and a grandmother shouting up the stairs.

## Neva — Norway

> "I am the oldest of three. My little brothers are twins and they are seven and
> they are never, ever quiet… My mother works at night…"

**Four people, and the twins are a blur.** Neva, calm, slightly apart.
**Twin brothers** [**Even** and **Elias**], 7, identical, in motion, causing
something. **Mother**, tired and warm, in a work coat, either arriving or
leaving.
**Setting:** a Norwegian timber house at the blue hour, snow through the windows, one lamp on, and two seven-year-olds wrecking the calm.

## Lenora — Mongolia

> "My brother Batu is eleven and he wants to win at everything, even at stars,
> which you cannot win. My father goes away for weeks with the horses…"

**Three people.** Lenora looking up; **Batu**, 11, also looking up and
competing about it; **Father**, in the middle distance with horses, because he
is the one who is away.
**Setting:** their ger at dusk, painted poles and bright hangings, horses and open steppe outside, enormous sky.

## Elowyn — New Zealand

> "My family is huge and everybody talks at the same time. My cousin Tane is
> exactly my age and we compete about everything. My nan is the one who tells
> the stories."

**Eight or more, all talking at once.** Elowyn; **Tane**, her age, opposite
her, rivalry visible; **Nan**, the still point, clearly the one being listened
to. Everyone else mid-conversation.
**Setting:** a great meeting house, full of her family and full of noise. Read the status through how the room defers to her nan, NOT through sacred dress. No korowai, no hei tiki, no pou.

## Clementine — United States

> "My little brother Sam is five and he leaves his football exactly where I need
> to stand… My dad burns everything he cooks… And my grandmother, who taught me
> and still tells me when I am wrong."

**Four people, and the football.** Clementine at the stove; **Sam**, 5, and
the football on the floor **exactly where she needs to stand**; **Dad**,
cheerful, something smoking behind him; **Grandmother**, arms folded, watching
Clementine's hands.
**Setting:** a big warm American kitchen in use, copper pans and mid-cooking chaos, Clementine in an apron with her sleeves up.

## Piper — Germany

> "I do not have any brothers or sisters… All my cousins are older than me and
> every single one of them plays something… My uncle taught me the violin and
> pretends he did not."

**No siblings.** Piper, the youngest and smallest, with **four or five older
cousins** [**Jonas**, **Mirela**, **Anton**, **Katrin**], every one holding an
instrument. **Uncle** [**Wim**] to one side, looking anywhere but at her.
**Setting:** a room full of instruments and open cases mid-gathering, and the youngest cousin holding the biggest one.

## Almasi — Kenya

> "My little sister Zuri is five and she asks why about four hundred times a
> day… My brother thinks digging is boring. He came out with me once and
> complained the entire time. But he came…"

**Three children.** Almasi; **Zuri**, 5, mid-question, hand up; **Brother**
[**Kip**], about 13, present but bored, not helping.
**Setting:** their home in Kenya, the dig visible beyond it, red dust everywhere. Almasi in her good clothes and covered in it.

## Bex — Brazil

> "My aunt brought me up and her house is completely full of things that do not
> work… My cousin Rafa is seventeen and he breaks things faster than I can fix
> them and he is still my favourite person in the world."

**Three people.** Bex with a screwdriver; **Aunt** [**Dona Lu**], warm,
surrounded by broken things she will not throw out; **Rafa**, 17, grinning,
holding something he has just broken.
**Setting:** a workshop that is also the living room, shelves of dead appliances, doors open to a warm street.

## The other two buttons

**My pet** — one animal, close, doing the thing the princess describes.
Clover mid-chew. Pebble half out of his shell. Gust in the air, never
perched. Wisp white in winter. Vesper on a post looking unimpressed.
Kōwhai the kea with her head in a bag. Biscuit under the table, hoping.
Tacet the blackbird on a wall, beak open. Digger the mongoose half buried,
tail up. Bolt the workshop cat sitting on exactly what Bex is working on.

**Life in my country** — no people needed, or only distant ones. The one
image a child would point at: the rooftops full of kites in Nepal, the pink
flamingo lake in Kenya, corn taller than a grown-up in America, the
midnight sun in Norway, flip-flop goalposts on a Brazilian street.
