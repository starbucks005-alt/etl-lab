# Store concept (brick and mortar)

Started 2026-10-03, from a conversation with Dr. O. This is a concept, not a plan.
Nothing here is decided unless it says so. Questions she has not answered are
written as questions. No prices yet: she said the thinking is about products first.

## Why it exists

The idea came from the old series The Waltons, and how the family said goodnight
to each other every night. A lonely person could say goodnight to their friends.

## What is sold

**Companions (the main shelf)**
- Each unit is one friend. A person owns as many as they like.
- Collectable means buying one every once in a while and saving up, not buying a
  set at once. Five was only an example number.
- Astra-9 Lite, the bench-top friend, runs on a Raspberry Pi now.
- Good Company friends, one character per unit, from the characters already written
  (The Dose, The Gym, Almost Human, Good Company). The AL panel already lets a unit
  take on any of 74 personalities, which could be how a customer picks which
  friend a unit becomes. That is not the same as one unit holding all of them.
- Bespoke friend: designed online at the Good Company build page, then collected in
  the store or shipped.
- Astra-9 and Astrad, full size: the mannequins arrive in about four weeks (as of
  2026-10-03) and are used to take orders and find funding and backers.

**Things that go with a friend**
- A stand, small shelf or bedside mount.
- Eye colours and accents, shown and chosen in the store.
- A collector's book or case, to record which friends a person owns.
- Gift packaging. The person buying for someone else may be the biggest customer.

**Other robots**
- A kitchen helper, a classroom helper and a workshop helper, matching the use
  cases on the Astra-9 page. Shown as concepts or taken as interest for now, the
  way the waitlist works.

**In the store**
- A meet-a-friend wall, to talk to any character before choosing.
- A design table, to build a bespoke friend with help.
- An AR corner showing a larger Astra-9 or Astrad on a screen.
- Set-up and repair help.

## Figurines on a plinth (idea, 2026-10-10)

Dr. O's idea: figurines, something like Barbie dolls, that talk when placed on a plinth
with a Raspberry Pi inside it. Dr. O tried the name vessels and it confused people
(2026-10-18), so they are called figurines. Her words:
"No, cannot work without plinth."
- The figurine is a collectable with no electronics of its own, apart from a small tag.
  The plinth holds the Pi, speaker and power, and reads the tag to know which
  character this is. Take her off and she is silent.
- The plinth is the starter product. Each figurine after that is a smaller purchase.
- One friend talks at a time on one plinth, which keeps the running cost down.
  Whether that is true is not known yet and needs the monthly cost measured.
- Children or adults depends on how the figurine is designed, in Dr. O's words. A
  suggestion, not a decision: the tag says which line a figurine belongs to and the
  plinth sets its behaviour to match, so one plinth can serve both.
  - Children's line: soft, safe and story-led. Does not ask for a name, address or
    school, and keeps no record of a child's voice. A parent can switch it off.
  - Adult line: open conversation with the Good Company personalities, and the
    goodnight ritual.
- Dr. O's idea for the first figurines: Little Astra-9 and Astrads, small versions of
  her own characters, for people who do not want life-size robots. Whether there
  are several Astrads, and whether they are the children's or adult line, is not
  answered yet.
- The figurine needs its own name and look. Barbie is Mattel's trademark and this
  should not copy it.

Open questions for the figurines:
1. Which line first, children's or adult?
2. Does the plinth ship with one figurine, or is the first figurine chosen at the counter?
3. Does a figurine work on any plinth, or is it paired to the buyer's?
4. How long is a plinth supported? Collectors will ask.
5. What stops a figurine's tag from being copied, if limited editions matter?
6. A children's figurine needs a lawyer to check toy safety rules and the rules on
   collecting information from children (COPPA in the US) before it is sold.

## ETL Security (idea, 2026-10-11)

Dr. O's idea: a security unit that knows everything about security and stands
guard, in a full-size body about the size of Astrad, seated at a desk in a lobby.
It does not need to act physically. The company already has doormen and security
guards that are not physical.
- Body: a full-size mannequin. Dr. O's photo is a black mannequin with a smooth
  oval, faceless head, dressed in a short-sleeve jacket, T-shirt and shorts. The
  head is to be painted to look like a security bot.
- Face: a smooth oval mask with no face. Ideas, not decided: a dark visor band, a
  visible camera lens, a light band or ring to show listening and speaking, a few
  fine panel lines. No police or military marks, and no paint over microphones.
- Camera: wanted, in Dr. O's words, "But with a camera". Suggested levels:
  1. Sees that someone is there and greets them. Low risk.
  2. Reads what a visitor shows it, such as a QR code, a badge or a typed name.
     Medium risk.
  3. Recognises faces. High risk. Some places, such as Illinois and the EU, have
     strict rules on biometrics. Treat it as off until a lawyer has checked the
     places it would be sold into.
  The camera lens should be visible, with a sign saying what it does and does not
  keep. Looking at things on the device and keeping little is the suggested default.
- Uniform: a jacket and shirt in a plain colour, with the building's name and a
  name tag. Name for the unit: "ETL Security", with a smaller line "AI front desk
  assistant". No badge number, and no insignia that looks like police.
- What it could do at a desk: greet and check in visitors, answer questions,
  know the building's security procedures, keep a visitor log, and cover quiet
  hours. It cannot stop anyone physically, so it calls a person when something
  looks wrong. It should not be described as a guard.

Open questions for ETL Security:
1. "ETL" is also Intertek's well-known "ETL Listed" electrical safety mark. A
   trademark lawyer should say whether "ETL Security" is a problem. Not known yet.
2. In some places, selling security guard services needs a licence, and a name or
   uniform can suggest it. Not known yet which places.
3. Is the camera only for greeting and check-in, or does it also watch the lobby?
4. Does the unit connect to a building's door entry and tenant lists?
5. Who is the first lobby: the store, a university, an office or an apartment block?
6. Does the oval carry a light or a screen, and does the unit get its own name?
7. How are visitors who are deaf or hard of hearing served? A small screen showing
   what it says is a suggestion.

## Companions that talk to each other (2026-10-11)

Dr. O: the holograms and robots can talk to each other, so a customer who buys
more than one plinth has companions that talk to each other. Her answers:
- They find each other on the same Wi-Fi.
- They talk whenever they like, not on a schedule.
- The owner can listen in, and can stop it.

What this could mean for the store (suggestions, not decisions):
- A goodnight round: each friend says goodnight to the others and then to the
  person. This is the idea that started the store.
- Each extra plinth adds conversation, so it gives a reason to buy more.
- The site already has 26 companion rooms, some with several companions in them.
  A room with several companions could be sold as a set.

Open questions:
1. Talking whenever they like can run up a cost without anyone asking for it. Not
   known how much. A daily limit and quiet hours at night are suggestions.
2. A children's-line friend should not join an adult-line friend's conversation.
   Suggestion: the figurine's tag decides who may talk to whom.
3. What do friends share about the owner when they talk, and what does each keep
   to itself? The owner should be able to see this and switch it off.
4. Being on the same Wi-Fi does not by itself say whether the conversations stay in
   the home. Where the talking is processed is not written down here yet.
5. A light on the plinth to show that friends are talking to each other, so the
   owner can tell.

## Plinth options: sensors (2026-10-18)

Dr. O: "the plinth can be with camera, with motion detector, or both, or none."
Her answer on which is standard: "things are optional." So there is no standard
sensor set. The customer chooses.
- None: talks and listens when spoken to.
- Motion detector: notices that someone has walked up and says hello. AL does this
  today with her own motion sensor.
- Camera: can see. The same three levels as ETL Security apply (notices someone is
  there, reads a code or badge shown to it, face recognition only after a lawyer has
  checked). Not decided.
- Both: the motion detector could wake the camera, so the camera is off most of
  the time. A suggestion, not a decision.

Suggestions, not decisions:
- Sensors are plug-in parts, so one can be added later.
- A light shows when the camera is on, and a physical switch or cover cuts it off.
- No camera on the children's line by default.
- A card in the box says what the camera does and does not keep.

Open questions:
1. Does a figurine's tag switch a sensor off, or does only the owner decide?
2. What does a camera module cost, and the motion sensor? The build document does
   not price the sensor, because it was bought in a shared pack. Not known yet.
3. For ETL Security and the Guardian units, is a camera expected, or can they be
   sold without one?

## What a plinth is made of (2026-10-18)

From the Astra-9 Lite build document (2026-10-04 and 2026-10-05). The Lite runs on a
Raspberry Pi 4 with 2GB. The Pi 5 boards Dr. O bought (about $200 each) are for the
full-size robots.
- Priced parts a plinth would need, one-off Amazon prices: Pi 4 bundle with card and
  case $65.11, power supply $9.66, 2-mic HAT $11.99, speakers and amplifier $7.99,
  cable $7.29, capacitor $4.99. About $107 in total.
- Not priced in the document and still needed: the tag reader, the light ring, the
  case with its speaker grille, wiring, assembly time, a shipping box and the figurine.
- Speech to text and the voice both come from ElevenLabs in the cloud, and the thinking
  from Anthropic's API, so the Pi is a thin device that listens, plays sound and runs
  the panel. Both cost money for every use.
- A suggestion, not a decision: build the first plinths on the Pi 4, which works.
  Then try a Pi Zero 2 W (about $15) with the same HAT, which should run the same
  software. Only if that fails look at a small voice board (about $24 to $35, not
  checked against a build), which needs the code rebuilt. The voice board figures
  come from web searches in October 2026 and are not confirmed.
- The running cost of the voice is likely to matter more than the parts. Not
  measured. One real week of use would show it.

## ETL Guardian (2026-10-18)

Dr. O shared ETL_Guardian.docx: a physical presence for a home, a business or a lab
that holds memory, notices unusual things, supports decisions, handles a crisis,
coordinates communication, keeps knowledge and understands human behaviour.
- AL already has a Guardian mode (added 2026-10-15, `al-panel/pi/al_skills.py` and
  `al_panel_server.py`): a front-door greeter. It greets people, gives directions from
  a building directory, points to forms, tells the front office who is here, keeps a
  downloadable visitor log, and says call 911 first when it hears emergency words.
  It never opens doors, blocks anyone, asks for ID or claims authority.
- Not built: memory (it is off in this mode), anything that sees (it has only a motion
  sensor), decision support, and crisis handling beyond telling people to call 911.
- ETL Security would be the lobby unit in this family. Whether ETL Guardian is the
  family name is not answered.
- Three points to resolve in the existing mode: its rules say to keep nothing about
  the people it speaks to, but it logs names, who they came to see and the time; the
  front-office notice is posted to a web address and includes the visitor's name;
  and speech to text often mishears names.
- Cautions: tracking a child's arrival or recognising visitors brings the biometric and
  children's data rules; a Guardian that coordinates emergencies should work beside
  certified alarms and a person, not replace them; guessing how someone feels is
  restricted in some workplaces; medical and family details are sensitive data; and the
  word "Guardian" itself suggests protection. A lawyer should check these.

## What the shop needs, and what is not proven (2026-10-18)

Suggestions from the Astra-9 session, not built unless stated:
- A real Talk button beside each robot, so it listens only when pressed. Reason: in a
  busy room a robot that listens to everyone hears the whole room and costs more in
  speech service credits. The Guardian at the door can always listen.
- A shop mode, like the Event mode: listening only on the button, short and
  child-safe answers, nothing remembered.
- One quiet switch for staff, to quiet every robot at closing time or when one says
  something odd.
- A sign saying what people hear and that nothing is kept without a parent's say.
- Robots for different people: a Children's friend at a child's height with a
  parent's notes, and a Companion or Science robot for grown-ups.

Turn-taking between robots is not proven. The Astra-9 session said it had treated it
as fixed, but Dr. O had told it several times that robots still talked over each
other, and no test has shown it solved. What is confirmed is narrower: Elle can reach
the shared "who has the floor" lock, and the stop phrase works. For the shop, the
simple protection is stations far apart with a Talk button on each. This also
affects the "companions that talk to each other" section above.

## A printer for figurines (2026-10-18)

Discussed with the Astra-9 session. The printer named is the Mimaki 3DUJ-2207, a
full-color UV 3D printer. Figures below are from Mimaki USA's spec sheet and
product page, MatterHackers, Aniwaa and 3DNatives, mostly 2022 and 2023, so get a
current quote:
- Build area 8 x 8 x 3 inches (203 x 203 x 76 mm), 28 micron layers, more than ten
  million colors, water-soluble support material, about 140 kg.
- Price: $46,286 from Mimaki USA (US and Canada), a list price of $50,652 at
  MatterHackers, and from about $42,076 at Aniwaa. MatterHackers says an installation
  and training session is required and costs another $7,000.
- The 3 inch height limit means a standing figurine taller than that would be printed
  lying down or in pieces. Whether the figurines are that tall is not decided.
- The Astra-9 session's suggestion: do not buy it first. Send one real figurine file
  to a print service that owns this printer, or ask Mimaki for sample prints, and use
  a service for the first 10 to 20 figurines. Price the printer only if orders keep
  coming, then think about space, installation and training.
- Open: what does a printed figurine cost per piece, and does the color quality
  suit a bespoke figurine? Not known yet.
- Dr. O sent this printer information to Vikram (2026-10-18). Nothing is signed.
  Anything sent to a print service or to the college should keep the figurine files
  and designs hers, and share only what is needed.

## Paid student internships (idea, 2026-10-18)

Dr. O: the shop/store could have paid internships for students to learn to program the
robots, build them, do AI agents and so on. This sits beside the business school
partnership. Nothing is decided or signed.

Possible tracks (suggestions): programming the robots; building (wiring, plinths,
painting, the LumVivo boxes); AI agents (characters, voices, rules); running the shop
(research, displays, sign-ups, sales numbers, customer questions); repair and set-up
help.

To settle before the first intern starts:
1. Who pays them: the shop, the school, a grant or a mix. Unpaid internships have legal
   limits, so paid is the safer way. Someone should confirm the rules for student
   workers.
2. Who owns the work. Code, designs and characters stay Dr. O's, and interns may not
   reuse or take them elsewhere. The agreement should say so.
3. What interns can see. Not customers' conversations or companions' memories,
   especially ME and any children's line.
4. Safety: soldering, power supplies and tools need supervision and a rule for who
   may use what.
5. Who supervises each intern, and what each is responsible for.
6. Insurance, and what changes if any intern is under 18.
A suggestion for a start: one short paid project, such as building and testing the Talk
button and quiet switch for the shop, to see what supervision takes.

## Cloud credits (idea, 2026-10-18)

Dr. O: the Azure and AWS startup programs give more credits if she partners with a
university. She is already in both programs (2026-10-18): the $5K credits tier on Azure, and
just started on AWS. The balances and expiry dates are not recorded. What was found (third-party summaries that disagree
on figures, so check the official pages):
- AWS Activate: a self-serve tier of roughly $1,000 to $5,000, and larger amounts
  (reported as $100,000 to $200,000) that need a partner such as an accelerator or
  incubator to refer the company. AWS announced on 2026-05-11 that Activate credits
  can pay for third-party models, including Anthropic's, on Amazon Bedrock.
- Microsoft for Startups Founders Hub: $1,000 to start, up to about $5,000 after
  business verification, and about $100,000 to $150,000 with an investor referral.
  The first $1,000 expires after 90 days and the rest 180 days after verification.
  A university itself is not eligible, so the credits would go to ETL.
- Not confirmed: that a university partnership alone raises the credits. The likelier
  route is a school incubator or innovation center that is a program partner and can
  give the referral. Whether WSU has one is not known.
- What credits would cover: Claude through Bedrock (the code calls Anthropic directly
  today and would change) and Azure speech to text. They would not pay for the
  ElevenLabs character voices, which are likely the biggest running cost. Not measured.
- Credits expire, so the timing should follow when they will be used.
- Azure speech to text includes 5 audio hours free a month and neural text to speech
  0.5 million characters free a month. The paid rates were not confirmed. A guess, not
  checked: about $1 per hour of speech to text and about $16 per million characters of
  neural voice, which would make a 30 minute session about $0.70, against $1.35 to $2.70
  for the voice alone on ElevenLabs. Check the Azure pricing page or calculator.
- The $5K Azure credits must be spent within about six months, roughly $800 a month,
  more than current use would spend, so the credits may run out of time before money.
- A suggestion: speech to text on Azure, an Azure voice for the Guardian and ETL Security
  units, ElevenLabs kept for the characters, and Claude on Bedrock tried in one mode.

Questions: what is left on each and when does it expire, how much did AWS give, and does
the business school have an incubator or innovation center that could give the
referral for a larger tier?

## Things to keep in mind

- Selling to lonely people is a trust business. Cancelling should be simple, nobody
  should lose their friend over a missed payment, and the owner should keep what
  they have built. These are suggestions, not decisions.
- Anything based on a real person's voice or likeness needs recorded consent first.
- Money taken for something not yet shipped needs clear terms: what, when, and the
  refund rule. A lawyer should check before any deposit is taken.
- Say what exists and what is a mannequin or a concept. The site already labels
  its images that way.
